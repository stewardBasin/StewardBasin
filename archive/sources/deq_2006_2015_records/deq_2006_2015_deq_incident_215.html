Incident Summary

Small amounts of mercury were discovered on the parking lot and sidewalk at the Vernal Middle School. Cause is unknown. Local Health Dept. and Fire Dept. have responded. Response: I have contacted the EPA who in turn will talk to the Tri County Health Dept. about the situation. If necessary, the EPA will hire their contractor -E.R.- to perform the cleanup. 09:12 TRI-County contacted DEQ again after learning of the possibility and availability of a mercury vapor meter. DERR sent some preliminary documentation with mercury info to the health dept. 10:20 Tri-County informed DEQ that an EPA OSC will mobilize to the school to render assistance. Tri-County is currently evacuating the school. Free mercury was not observed at the entrances used by students. Free mercury was believed observed near where the cooks enter the kitchen. 11:30 by JD Keetley: Craig Meyers (303-312-7166), OSC with EPA, informed me that he has sent the EPA responder, David Romero (303-884-1236) to Vernal along with the EPA contractor, URS. They are driving to Vernal from Denver and should arrive there in a few hours. Any questions on the response can be taken up with them. I’ll update things as necessary. – JD 8/25/06 Update by JD Keetley: The EPA and contractor URS arrived in Vernal Thursday night and the cleanup is underway. Friday morning (8/25/06) I received an inquiry from Richard Gee with State Public Safety and Darren Brown with Tri-County Health Department regarding State regulations or guidelines for the cleanup of mercury inside the Vernal school. I told them we have guidelines that are based on the U.S. EPA’s guidelines taken off their website. After conferring with other DERR personnel and with SLVHD personnel who have had experience with similar situations, I called back Darren Brown and advised that the usual cleanup level for mercury in ambient air is 3,000 nanograms/cubic meter for a commercial building and 1,000 nanograms/cubic meter for a residential building. I added that a public school would be better off to make the cleanup level 1,000 nanograms (1 microgram). Darren told me they had already made that decision and were going ahead with that cleanup level in mind. He said the highest readings on their Lumex machine have been around 400 - 500 nanograms and that staying under 1,000 nanograms should not be that difficult. There was still some uncertainty as to whether the cleanup would address the cafeteria workers who had possibly tracked the mercury onto their shoes and into their cars and homes. If the EPA demobilizes after cleaning up the school only, then the Tri-County Health Department would be left to handle the monitoring and cleanup of the workers’ personal clothing and homes. Since the County does not have a Lumex meter, they would ask DERR’s assistance in doing this since we do have a Lumex meter and trained personnel to use it. As of 11:00 am Friday, no decision has yet been reached regarding the potential cafeteria workers’ mercury contamination. - JDK Update & Summary 8/29/06: Thursday, August 24, 2006: Mercury contamination was discovered in the parking and inside the building at the Vernal Middle School due to an anonymous phone call in the morning. All students, faculty, and staff were dismissed from the school. Initial notification of the incident to UDEQ/DERR was received Thursday morning around 8:30. The situation was discussed with both Richard Gee at Utah DES and Darren Brown with Tri-County Health Department. At 9:00 am, Craig Meyers with the U.S. EPA was contacted. Craig later informed that he had mobilized their contractor, URS, and his field OSC, Dave Romero, to go to the site. They arrived in Vernal late in the afternoon and began initial assessment and cleanup of the site. It was estimated about 3 tablespoons of mercury had been spilled outside in the parking lot, and school employees later tracked the mercury into the building. Mercury vapors were detected inside the school building. The contractor ventilated the building, began clean up the mercury using special vacuums, and prepared to remove the contaminated flooring and carpets if necessary. Friday August 25: Cleanup levels for the mercury spill were discussed. An “action level” of 1,000 nanograms/cubic meter (n-gm/m3 or 10-9 gm/m3) of mercury vapor in the air was set. The contractor used a Lumex meter to measure mercury levels as cleanup of the school interior and the outside parking lots and sidewalks progressed. Most levels inside the school were found to be less than 500 n-gm/m3, while outside levels of 1,000+ n-gm/m3were measured. School was still not in session during the cleanup. Meanwhile, the State DOH and the Utah Poison Control Center had been getting calls from concerned parents and doctors in Vernal about the spill. The issue of the cafeteria workers’ possible mercury contamination on their clothes, homes, or cars was brought up. In the afternoon, the EPA, the URS contractor, and the Tri-County Health Department offered employees of the school who feel they may have come into contact with mercury to have their clothes, shoes, cars, etc tested for mercury. It was a voluntary offer, and EPA/URS would be in town all day Friday for it. One employee came to be tested, and had her car tested for mercury levels. The car’s levels were below action levels. It was felt this car was the one most likely to have been exposed to mercury since it had been parked in the area of the spill in the parking lot. By Friday evening, the cleanup was mostly over. All contaminated materials were placed in bags and secured for the weekend. The contractor continued to ventilate the school overnight and planned to come back in the morning to test the ambient air levels. Saturday August 26: When the contractor returned to the school in the morning, the highest level of mercury measured was 250 n-gm/m3, found in the janitor’s closet due to an initial attempt to cleanup the spill himself. Throughout the rest of the building, all rooms were tested and found to be at acceptable levels. Outside, levels of 1,000 n-gm/m3 were still detected so the contractor applied MercEx (?) to the sidewalk: sprinkling finely powdered sulfur or zinc on the spill site to bind any remaining mercury so it can no longer vaporize. All cracks and crevices in the sidewalks were then sealed with caulking. When the sidewalks were tested again, results showed levels below 200 n-gm/m3. The EPA and URS returned to Denver Saturday afternoon. The contaminated materials from the cleanup were double-bagged and placed in a steel container which was later moved to a vacant, secure 5-acre lot owned by the Health Department. The contractor took samples of the contaminated material to deliver to a laboratory in Steamboat Springs, Colorado, for analysis to determine if the material is hazardous. When results come back, URS will return to Vernal to retrieve the container and dispose of it in an appropriate landfill: either Subtitle C or D depending if the material is deemed hazardous or not. The material will be hauled off within a week. Monday August 28: Health Department personnel met with school officials and verified that everyone is satisfied with the cleanup. School is back in session and there are no further issues of concern. For any further information on this incident, please contact J. D. Keetley with UDEQ at 801-536-4130 or at jkeetley@utah.gov
