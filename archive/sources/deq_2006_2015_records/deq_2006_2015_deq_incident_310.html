Incident Summary

From John Isanhart USFS 11/8/11: Looks like it is mostly cleaned up. The pit has oil in it with a lot of cellulose. It really only went between the well head and pit.. No signs of spillage outside of the pad area. This is a fee well so our jurisdiction is just mig birds and other listed fish or animals. The only listed species in the near vicinity would be Ute's ladies tresses. There are some wetland areas along the road into the well but not next to the pad itself. The well is in the NENW Sec 3 T 2 S R 3 W. It is labelled the Conover 3-3B-3. Update From Scott Hacking: 11/5/2011 - I was at the site today at noon. El Paso was right in the middle of critical operations to contain the well, so I was not able to get too close to the well head (still a threat of explosion, H2S gas present). The well was still uncontained but oil was not really shooting up in the air, the oil had been diverted to flow to excavated ponds on site. As far as I could tell oil was not leaving the site. El Paso did have a stream of trucks coming and going to truck the oil out of the emergency pits that had been constructed at the site. The nearest downstream surface water is a farmer's pond about 1/4 mile away. The first "live" water is about two miles SE downstream (Dry Gulch Creek, which does have water at this location). There is also a pond that is closer to the NW but that pond is uphill from the site. The irrigation season is over so the farmer's ditches and the canals do have water in them currently. It is unknown how deep the groundwater is at the site, but apparently they did not hit groundwater while excavating the emergency ponds. Dwayne Davies with El Paso is the contact for this incident, he was at the site but was extremely busy with operations to cap the well. His mobile phone is 435-823-1470, and his e-mail is dwayne.davies@elpaso.com. EPA was not at the site yet when I was there. The local incident command system has not been activated, El Paso is in control of the site and has the resources to address the situation. They have established access control at the site and appear to be dealing with the situation appropriately and with urgency. A well control company (Wild Well Control) has been brought in to help them deal with the situation. El Paso has multiple H2S meters at the site and on their personnel. The nearest neighbor is about 1/8 mile to the west (see photo 16 in background), which is generally upwind but not always (today the wind was blowing stiffly to the east so that neighbor was upwind). The next nearest potentially downwind neighbor is about 1/4 mile to the NE (see photo 15, the residence is on the right). There are two other residences to the NW and SW slightly further away. There did not appear to be anybody home at the two closest residences, they may have been advised to leave by Mike Lefler (local Fire Marshall), but I am not sure about that. So there appears to be no release to surface water at this time. There is still some potential for H2S gas release, but measurements so far at the site (according to El Paso) have been low. The wind is helping disperse H2S from the immediate area. We should revisit the site Monday or Tuesday (hopefully the well is fully contained by then) for more detailed followup and to determine for sure if the excavated ponds encountered any groundwater. I don't forsee that water sampling will be needed unless we find out GW was impacted at the site. - Scott Scott D. Hacking, P.E.
